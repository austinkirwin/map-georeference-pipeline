# Historical zoning research scope

The central question—“what zoning ordinance applied to this location on a historical date?”—cannot be answered safely from a map overlay alone.

## Required evidence chain

1. **Historical location**: normalize the historic address or parcel and document how it maps to a geometry.
2. **Jurisdiction at the date**: determine whether the site was within a municipality, county, township, or unincorporated area at the relevant time.
3. **Controlling law**: locate the ordinance/version in effect on that date and its adoption/effective dates.
4. **Adopted map**: locate the official zoning map or a source that identifies the district boundary.
5. **Spatial relationship**: use a reviewed georeference to assess whether the historical location lies in the documented district geometry.
6. **Uncertainty statement**: record map scale, boundary ambiguity, annexation, address change, transform accuracy, and missing evidence.
7. **Human review**: distinguish a supported research finding from an authoritative legal conclusion.

## Result categories

- **Supported**: a reviewer can trace the outcome to a cited controlling ordinance, adopted map, historical jurisdiction, and adequately documented spatial relationship.
- **Inconclusive**: evidence exists but is incomplete, conflicting, or too uncertain to support a conclusion.
- **Not found**: no relevant source was located; this is not evidence that no zoning rule existed.

## Use of AI

AI can help propose landmarks, transcribe legible labels, extract candidate ordinance references, and flag inconsistencies. It cannot authenticate a source, select the controlling law, resolve a legal ambiguity, or replace reviewer approval. AI-suggested material must be labeled as such and linked to the evidence a human used to verify it.
